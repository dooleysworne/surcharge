How the route-share calculation works

The landfill trip is treated as a shared route expense. This calculator assigns the neighborhood a percentage of the landfill round-trip based on its percentage of the route's customers. Miles driven inside the neighborhood are assigned entirely to that neighborhood.

Shared-route values include landfill travel allocated by customer share. Neighborhood-only values reflect fuel usage for collection inside the neighborhood alone. This report is intended to provide a transparent breakdown of fuel-related costs attributable to residential waste collection.

Important Assumptions

This is an estimate of fuel cost only. It does not attempt to calculate labor, truck depreciation, maintenance, insurance, disposal/tipping fees, administrative costs, profit, taxes, or other components of a company's fuel surcharge.

The shared‑route calculations reflect fuel usage allocated to this neighborhood based on its proportion of total route customers, including landfill travel. The neighborhood‑only calculations show the fuel required to service the neighborhood itself, without considering landfill distance or shared-route allocation. Both values are provided to help illustrate how local collection and route-wide travel contribute differently to overall fuel costs.

Most rear‑loader garbage trucks serving local neighborhoods use compacting bodies in the 20–25 cubic‑yard range, even when the overall vehicle length appears closer to 30–35 feet. Modern rear loaders compress household waste aggressively, allowing a truck of this size to service roughly 700–1,000 homes per load under typical residential conditions. Actual capacity varies with route density, cart size, and the type of waste collected, but this range provides a realistic benchmark for understanding how landfill travel and neighborhood mileage contribute to fuel usage on a shared route.

The default 2.6 MPG is intentionally conservative for a residential diesel side-loader. A real-world study of six diesel rear-loader refuse trucks reported an average of 2.6 MPG, while other refuse-truck categories can have materially different fuel economy.

A U.S. Department of Energy summary gives a broader 4.5 MPG figure for refuse trucks in 2021, illustrating why the actual truck and duty cycle matter. The calculator therefore lets you change the MPG assumption.
